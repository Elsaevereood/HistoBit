**Iran** just fired anti-ship missiles at two American destroyers in the **Gulf of Oman**, and Washington's response will define the credibility of U.S. naval power for the next decade.

## Details of the Iran Attack on USS Truxtun and USS Mason

On June 5, 2026, **Iran's regular Navy** launched **Qadir** anti-ship cruise missiles and **Shahed Danesh** loitering munitions at **USS Truxtun (DDG-103)** and **USS Mason (DDG-87)** operating in international waters. Tehran claimed the attack was a defensive response to American harassment of Iranian oil tankers and asserted that U.S. ships were forced to retreat toward the **Indian Ocean**. **CENTCOM** denied everything, calling Iran's claims deliberate psychological warfare. That same day, the U.S.-brokered **Lebanon** ceasefire collapsed completely. **Hezbollah's** Secretary-General **Naim Qassem** rejected withdrawal terms as humiliating, and **Israel** confirmed it is permanently holding its Lebanese buffer zone, including the strategically positioned **Beaufort Castle**, with no plan to withdraw.

<FactBox>
The June 5 attack was carried out by Iran's conventional Navy, not the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. The IRGC handles most Iranian provocations at sea. Deploying regular naval forces against Aegis-equipped American destroyers signals that Tehran is prepared to escalate beyond proxy harassment into direct military confrontation.
</FactBox>

## The Nuclear Deadlock Driving Iran's Military Escalation

This is direct fallout from **Operation Epic Fury**, the **February 2026** joint U.S.-Israel campaign that killed **Supreme Leader Khamenei**. His replacement adopted a maximalist survival posture: lock the **Strait of Hormuz**, accelerate uranium enrichment, and order proxies to concede nothing. The **IAEA** confirmed Iran blocked international inspectors for nearly a year, leaving near-weapons-grade uranium stockpiles entirely unaccounted for. Nuclear back-channel talks in **Pakistan** are paralyzed. Trump says a deal is days away. Tehran says no progress has been made.

## Why the US Navy Response in the Persian Gulf Matters

Iran targeted **Aegis**-equipped American destroyers with precision anti-ship missiles and faced no kinetic response. If that calculus holds, every state with those weapons just learned that U.S. naval deterrence in the Gulf has a ceiling. The Middle East is consuming the interceptors and carrier groups **NATO** cannot afford to lose anywhere else.
